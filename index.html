Where Florida's water goes

Maps of Florida's rivers, springs, and sinks, from the springs belt to the St. Lucie, built from USGS, FDEP, and Florida Geological Survey data.

Where does the rain go?Every mapped creek from the middle Suwannee to Rainbow River and the Ocklawaha, up the St. Johns to its headwaters, and down to the St. Lucie and the Loxahatchee, colored by whether it reaches the Gulf, the Atlantic, or the aquifer, and followed down the rivers to the sea. The Santa Fe breathes groundwaterRiver and spring flow, the underground stretch at O'Leno, and the aquifer from before development to today. Florida's springsEvery spring on the state's list, sized by how much water it puts out, with the springsheds its cleanup plans cover. Rainbow River starts full grownA river that rises out of the ground at full size: its springs, the springshed that feeds them, the Withlacoochee it joins, and sixty years of flow. Lake Okeechobee used to drain southCanals now take Florida's biggest lake west, east, and south. Live flow at its outlets, and where its water has gone since 1932. The St. Lucie was plumbed to a lakeA century-old canal ties the St. Lucie River to Lake Okeechobee. Live flow at both ends of the canal, the estuary's salinity, and lake releases since 1931.
